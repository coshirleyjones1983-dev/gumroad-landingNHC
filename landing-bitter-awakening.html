What does your blood sugar actually do after a meal?

The sixty to ninety minutes after a carbohydrate-containing meal decide most of what comes after — including the familiar afternoon crash. Tap below to compare a flattened response with the pattern most people live with.

At the meal 90 min after 3 hrs after

The target response: blood sugar rises gently after eating and eases back down smoothly, with no crash to chase behind it. The pattern this book opens with: a hard spike in the ninety minutes after eating, followed by a drop below baseline — the mechanism behind the familiar 3pm crash.

One cascade, four doors

Five symptoms. One system falling out of rhythm.

I

Insulin resistance takes the wheel

Cells stop responding efficiently to insulin, glucose lingers in the blood, and the pancreas overcompensates — itself a signal to store fat, independent of what's actually being eaten.

II

The gut's own fullness signal goes quiet

The body's natural satiety hormone is released in response to bitter and fibrous foods specifically — a diet built on sweet, salty, and umami gives that pathway almost nothing to respond to.

III

The hunger hormone overcorrects

Poor sleep and irregular eating push its baseline higher, especially in the evening — driving cravings for exactly the foods an already-struggling system handles worst.

IV

Cortisol rewrites where fuel goes

Chronic stress hormone elevation directs fat to the midsection and breaks down muscle for fuel — lowering metabolic rate and making every other door harder to close.
